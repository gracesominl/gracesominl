Hi there! I’m a Master’s student in Computational Social Science at <a href="https://kaist.ac.kr/en/" style="color: #f5628d; text-decoration: none;">KAIST</a>, where I work with <a href="https://tds-lab.vercel.app/" style="color: #f5628d; text-decoration: none;">The Society Lab</a>. I’m interested in how people collectively produce new boundaries between humans and nonhumans. Recently, I’ve been studying the sociotechnical imaginaries of AI that emerge in everyday public discourse, and how these imaginaries lay the groundwork for rethinking human–AI boundaries.

Before turning to this line of research, I worked in the humanitarian and international development field, where I was driven by a desire to make underconceptualized problems in our society more visible. That experience has led me to see computational methods not simply as technical tools, but as a powerful interpretive language through which complex social problems are made legible to wider publics.

What fundamentally connects my past to the present is my interest in the power of discourse: how language connects my world and yours, and in doing so, creates new possibilities for coevolving with technology. We each inhabit different corners of reality, but through language, whether spoken, written, embodied, or encoded, we can allow those realities to unfold through shared interpretation. If you’d like to share your side of the world, feel free to reach out. I’d be glad to begin another small yet meaningful discourse!
